**September 21–23, 2026 | Hangzhou, China**

From September 21 to 23, 2026, MindsLeap delivered an AI study tour in Hangzhou for the Tsinghua SEM EMBA 25D class. The program connected frontier AI technology visits with sessions on AI-native organizations, hands-on construction of an entrepreneur AI second brain, and enterprise GEO strategy.

The study tour treated AI as more than a technology topic. It followed a practical sequence: see real technologies, understand organizational change, and build personal and enterprise capabilities through hands-on work.

## Seeing AI in Real-World Contexts at Rokid and BrainCo

The tour included visits to Rokid (灵伴科技) and BrainCo (强脑科技). These visits gave participants the opportunity to observe AI from the perspectives of products, interaction, and industry applications: from new forms of human-computer interaction through AI glasses to brain-computer interfaces and intelligent hardware.

![Tsinghua SEM EMBA 25D participants experience AI glasses at Rokid](/images/news/tsinghua-emba-hangzhou-ai-study-tour-2026-rokid.jpg)

At Rokid, participants experienced AI glasses and related spatial-computing scenarios. New interaction models also raise a management question: when information can be called more naturally, are an enterprise’s knowledge, workflows, and services ready to be reorganized by AI?

![Tsinghua SEM EMBA 25D participants visit BrainCo to learn about brain-computer interfaces and intelligent hardware](/images/news/tsinghua-emba-hangzhou-ai-study-tour-2026-brainco.jpg)

At BrainCo, participants examined how frontier technologies become products, how products create real user experiences, and how new technology opens up industry opportunities. The value of the visit was not only learning about one technology company, but also placing AI concepts back into concrete product and user contexts.

## Why Enterprises Need to Understand AI-Native Organizations

After the visits, Lincoln Wang led a session on how to build an AI-native organization. The focus was not how many AI tools an enterprise owns, but whether AI enters the organization’s ways of working, knowledge systems, workflow design, and decision mechanisms.

Traditional enterprises often rely on employee experience, meetings, approvals, and informal coordination. An AI-native organization reorganizes employee capability, enterprise information, and business workflows so that Skills become callable assets, Memory becomes organizational memory, Workflows carry collaboration between people and Agents, and Agents can execute within clear boundaries.

![Lincoln Wang shares how to build an AI-native organization during the Tsinghua SEM EMBA Hangzhou AI study tour](/images/news/tsinghua-emba-hangzhou-ai-study-tour-2026-ai-native-organization.jpg)

This means that AI transformation is not finished when a tool training session ends. It begins when an organization starts to redefine tasks, roles, context, checkpoints, and accountability.

## Building an Entrepreneur AI Second Brain

In the hands-on session, participants used their own work, industries, and enterprise questions as inputs to build an entrepreneur AI second brain. The work included identifying knowledge that should be retained, defining tasks AI could support, designing how context would be retrieved, and setting boundaries for fact-checking and human judgment.

![Tsinghua SEM EMBA participants work together on an entrepreneur AI second brain](/images/news/tsinghua-emba-hangzhou-ai-study-tour-2026-second-brain.jpg)

An AI second brain is not simply a collection of files inside a tool. It is a knowledge system in which personal experience, enterprise information, and work processes can be retrieved, called, and continuously updated. For entrepreneurs, it can support research, decision preparation, meeting reviews, content production, and business collaboration, while leaving critical judgments to people.

## Using Enterprise GEO to Become Understandable to AI

The study tour also examined enterprise GEO strategy. For a modern enterprise, the official website is not only a brand showcase. It should also become a factual foundation that search engines and AI models can understand, cite, and verify.

Enterprise GEO is not about stacking keywords. It is about answering a set of concrete questions consistently: who the enterprise is, what it provides, which organizations it has served, what types of problems it addresses, which content and images serve as evidence, and whether the website, topic pages, news, and external channels form a coherent entity relationship.

That is why the program discussed AI-native organizations, AI second brains, and GEO together. Enterprises need to use AI, but they also need to express their capabilities in a structured and verifiable way that can enter the knowledge space AI systems understand.

## From a Study Tour to Organizational Action

The three-day program connected technology visits, management discussion, and hands-on practice. Participants first observed how AI is entering products and everyday life at Rokid and BrainCo, then returned to questions about internal workflows, knowledge assets, and management practices, and finally translated those insights into personal and enterprise next steps through the AI second brain and GEO exercises.

For organizations pursuing enterprise AI transformation, the starting point is usually not choosing the “strongest model.” It is clarifying business goals, organizational capability, knowledge assets, and the workflows that need to be redesigned.

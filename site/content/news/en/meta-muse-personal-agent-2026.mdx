**Author: Lincoln Wang | Founder & CEO, MindsLeap | Partner and CEO, Founders Space China | Founder, MindsLeap Founders AI Club**

If an AI assistant only answers questions, it is closer to a search box. When it can open a browser, fill in a form, schedule a meeting, or complete a purchase, it begins to resemble an assistant in the real sense of the word.

Meta's Muse is pushing personal AI in that direction. It is designed to work with schedules, shopping, email, travel planning, and long-term goals. At Connect, Meta also described a path into computers, smart glasses, email, and third-party services.

The important development is not another AI application. It is that a personal agent is receiving the entrances through which it can act. The request is no longer only “What should I do?” It is becoming “Please finish this for me.”

## The Assistant Is Moving from Answering to Executing

When Muse launched in the United States in early September, Meta's description had already moved beyond a conventional chatbot. A user can give it a goal, and Muse can help break that goal into a plan, coordinate time and resources, and continue the task. The Associated Press reported that Muse can open browsers, fill forms, and negotiate on a user's behalf.

That is different from asking an assistant to draft an email. In the traditional model, the user still clicks Send. A personal agent tries to understand the context, find the right page, complete the operation, and return with the result.

Meta says Muse runs inside a dedicated secure virtual machine and emphasizes the separation of user data and agent activity. It can be used through a dedicated Muse application and through WhatsApp.

The product path is straightforward: let the user express a goal in natural language, give the agent limited tools and permissions, and put execution inside an environment that can be monitored.

## The Tool List Is Getting Longer

Meta Connect made Muse's direction more visible. TechCrunch reported that Meta is adding Mac capabilities so users can delegate computer tasks and step away while Muse works.

Muse is also moving toward Meta's smart glasses. Users can speak to it while asking for help with exercise planning, food logs, service bookings, or purchases based on something they see. Meta has also described digital avatars and a dedicated email direction, giving Muse more ways to receive tasks and work over time.

Connectors may be even more important. Meta announced integrations with Stripe, Shopify, PayPal, GitHub, and Notion, along with retailers including Best Buy, Gap, Sephora, Walmart, and Wayfair. TechCrunch reported that Meta said it received more than 1,500 developer connector applications in a week.

The strategic asset may therefore not be one chat interface. It may be how many real systems the agent can enter. The more useful a personal agent becomes, the more it needs calendars, email, payments, shopping, and work tools. Its practical boundary is set by those connections.

## Amazon's Pushback Brings the Problem into the Real Economy

Less than two weeks after Muse launched, Amazon blocked it from accessing its store and buying products for users. Axios reported that Amazon said third-party applications must respect whether a service provider allows that kind of participation, and that Amazon had not authorized Muse to access accounts, scrape data, or process transactions.

This is not merely a compatibility issue. It exposes the central commercial conflict around personal agents. Users want one agent to complete a task across platforms. Platforms want to retain the customer relationship, transaction entry point, and behavioral data.

If a user discovers a product, compares prices, and buys it through Muse, that user may open Amazon less often. From Amazon's perspective, Muse is not just a shopping tool; it could become a new intermediary taking over the entrance to the marketplace.

Meta faces a corresponding problem. It wants Muse to become a user's digital representative, but every action requires authorization from another platform. The more proactive the agent becomes, the more clearly platforms have to answer: who is it, whom does it represent, and who is responsible when it is wrong?

## Trust Has to Accumulate One Action at a Time

The more an AI assistant knows and can do, the more convenience it provides, but the cost of trust rises with it.

Muse may need access to schedules, email, preferences, and long-term goals. It may run in the background and handle several tasks. Users do not simply need a system that can do everything. They need to know what it accessed, what it did, what went wrong, and which steps must return to a person for confirmation.

Meta's secure virtual machine and privacy design are necessary for entering daily life, but they are not the entire answer. Permission management, action logs, undo mechanisms, payment confirmation, and third-party authorization will determine whether people are willing to delegate.

The same is true in enterprise AI. An agent that can read customer records, modify a CRM, and send a quote cannot be judged only by how intelligent its answer sounds. A company must know which permissions it has, why it took an action, who can review it, and how an error can be recovered.

## The Next Platform Contest May Be About Letting Agents In

Platforms once competed for whether users opened their applications. As personal agents spread, the competition may become whether a platform lets an external agent enter, and what that agent is allowed to do.

If every platform closes its doors, an agent cannot complete cross-platform work. If every platform opens everything, it may lose control of the user experience, data, and transaction. A more complicated layer of agent identity, permissions, and payment protocols is likely to follow.

That creates a more concrete opportunity than building another chat assistant: identity for agents, permission management, action audits, transaction confirmation, and cross-platform coordination. For a personal agent to work on a person's behalf, it needs an infrastructure of trust behind it.

## What This Means for Enterprise Builders

Muse is still early and is initially focused on the United States, with many capabilities rolling out gradually. But its direction is clear: the personal AI assistant is moving from a window that talks with you to an agent that works across multiple systems.

The real question is not only whether an agent can complete a task. It is whether, after completing it, the user still understands what happened and can reclaim the final decision.

The destination for personal agents may not be an AI that feels more human. It may be a relationship in which people can delegate with confidence. Capability determines what the agent can do. Permission determines where it can go. Trust determines how much of life a person is willing to hand over.

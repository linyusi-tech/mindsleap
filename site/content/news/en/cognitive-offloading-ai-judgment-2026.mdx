This article was adapted by Lincoln from the MindsLeap AI Insights short video "Cognitive Offloading: Why AI Users Still Need to Practice Judgment", published on September 5, 2026.

Andrew Ng has used the term `cognitive offloading` to describe a risk that grows as AI becomes more useful. People do not only hand tools repetitive tasks; they can also start handing over the work of comparison, verification, and judgment.

In this episode, Lincoln Wang discusses three ways to keep the human role active: ask an Agent to compare alternatives rather than return a single answer, use AI to support learning rather than replace the learning process, and keep practicing independent judgment in fields where experience determines how outputs should be checked.

The practical takeaway is not to use less AI. It is to give AI explicit context and quality standards while retaining responsibility for the final decision.
